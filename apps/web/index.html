What a report answers

What is it?
A dataset of episodes, each hashed to a leaf in the log — the corpus root fixes exactly which episodes are included.
Where did it come from?
Who captured it, when, and under what terms — signed before it was logged, so the leaf itself is the evidence.
Who may train on it?
The terms hash is anchored with the corpus, and a licence receipt on chain names the root, the terms and the amount paid.
Is that still true?
Consent status is proven against a revocation root committed the same way as the data, checkable at the moment you read it.

The four badges

Badges over an L0 base, not a cumulative ladder. Every surface shows the set an episode holds, worded exactly like this.

L0Committed

Committed — existed by block {block} on {chain}; log unchanged since; consent {live | revoked at block {onset}} as of anchor (size {size}).

L1Signed

Signed — {org} signed this record before it was logged; unchanged since.

L2Attested

Attested — signing key held in hardware attested by {manufacturer} ({model}); this proves which device signed, not what its sensors saw.

L3Checked

Checked by {operator} — {n} checks run: {list}. Heuristic; see details.

Source: declared or attested

Badges answer whether the bytes are what the signer says. Source is a separate claim: what kind of thing produced them.

What this does not prove

  • The operator (THENAR) can decline to log or anchor a record; append receipts and public audit make this detectable, not impossible.
  • Checks are heuristics with recorded thresholds; they can be evaded and can err; they are evidence, not proof.
  • A signature proves which key signed, not what a sensor measured; captured_at, source and embodiment are claims by the signer.
  • Consent onset is recorded; what a buyer may do after onset is governed by the terms document, not by this protocol.
  • Anchors depend on the availability of at least one chain carrying the log; the same log is anchored on more than one.

What this proves

Every leaf in this register was committed to an append-only log before it was anchored, and the anchor is public and checkable against the chain.

What it does not

A badge proves the record, not the sensor. Source is a claim by the signer unless the attested-physical conditions hold.

How to check it yourself: three commands

  1. Read the anchor from GraspLog on Avalanche Fuji.
  2. Recompute the leaf and walk the inclusion proof to that root.
  3. Recompute the consent leaf and walk it to the revocation root.
