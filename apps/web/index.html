What a report answers

Four questions
counsel asks

A Provenance Report answers the questions a buyer's legal team asks before licensing a dataset. Every answer is checkable without trusting THENAR.

What is it?

A dataset of episodes: the files, their hashes, metadata, and the range of episodes included. Verifiable against the corpus root.

Where did it come from?

Who captured each episode, when, under what terms and consent status. Signatures prove identity at submission. Revocation is provable if consent was withdrawn later.

Who may train on it?

The license terms are recorded with the dataset and anchored on chain. Supplier and terms are immutable. Buyers get a receipt naming the root, terms, and amount paid.

Is that still true?

The log is append-only. Consistency proofs show nothing was rewritten since the anchor. Revocation status is proven at the moment of purchase or delivery.

Claim levels (PLAN §1)

Every surface shows
what each badge proves

Levels are badges over an L0 base, not a cumulative ladder. Each episode carries the set of badges it holds. Wording is verbatim and never weakened.

L0 Committed

Existed by block {block} on {chain}; log unchanged since; consent {live | revoked at block {onset}} as of anchor (size {size}).

L1 Signed

{org} signed this record before it was logged; unchanged since.

L2 Attested

Signing key held in hardware attested by {manufacturer} ({model}); this proves which device signed, not what its sensors saw.

L3 Checked

Checked by {operator} — {n} checks run: {list}. Heuristic; see details.

Known limitations (PLAN §22)

What it does not prove

The following is stated in every report and never weakened:

  • The operator (THENAR) can decline to log or anchor a record; append receipts and public audit make this detectable, not impossible.
  • Checks are heuristics with recorded thresholds; they can be evaded and can err; they are evidence, not proof.
  • A signature proves which key signed, not what a sensor measured; captured_at, source and embodiment are claims by the signer.
  • Consent onset is recorded; what a buyer may do after onset is governed by the terms document, not by this protocol.
  • Anchors depend on the availability of at least one chain carrying the log; the same log is anchored on more than one.

Research, not products

Physical AI and settlement

Provenance you can check
without trusting us

Contact data is only worth licensing if a buyer's legal team can trace where it came from and on what terms. This is the mechanism, including the part a ledger does not solve.

Only hashes go on chain

The recording and the contributor's identity never touch it. What gets written is a hash of the payload and the licence terms agreed at capture. That is what keeps erasure possible: delete the payload and the record still proves what the terms were, while holding nothing personal. An immutable public log of who recorded what in their own kitchen would be the opposite of handling consent well.

Payment and provenance, one entry

Each accepted clip settles against the same record that carries its terms. Not because paying people needs a chain, because it does not: Wise and Stripe Connect pay large populations small amounts every day and would not ask a contributor to hold a wallet. The reason is that it collapses the receipt and the provenance into a single auditable thing instead of two systems somebody has to reconcile.

What a ledger does not do

It attests record integrity after submission, not the truth of content at capture. Nothing written on chain proves a stream came from a hardware-captured source rather than a replay, and a device key can be extracted. Solving that needs attestation in the hardware, which is a secure element and a remote attestation path we have not built. We would rather say so here than be asked in diligence.

If a plainer rail does it, we use it

Signed capture manifests in a public append only log give an auditor the same tamper evidence, with tooling they already know from certificate transparency. We are not attached to the word. We are attached to a buyer being able to verify a slice without taking our internal records on faith, and to a contributor being able to check their own terms without asking us.
