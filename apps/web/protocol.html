The claim ladder

Five levels of evidence, each with fixed wording

Levels are badges, not a cumulative ladder. The only implication is L2 ⇒ L1. Every surface shows the set of badges an episode holds and uses this wording verbatim.

L0Committed

Committed — existed by block {block} on {chain}; log unchanged since; consent {live | revoked at block {onset}} as of anchor (size {size}).

L1Signed

Signed — {org} signed this record before it was logged; unchanged since.

L2Attested

Attested — signing key held in hardware attested by {manufacturer} ({model}); this proves which device signed, not what its sensors saw.

L3Checked

Checked by {operator} — {n} checks run: {list}. Heuristic; see details.

—Pending

Pending — received, not yet anchored. (not a level)

—Check failed

Check {name} failed: {summary}. (appended whenever any claim is fail)

Every badge wording is fixed and never weakened. The protocol never claims authenticity at capture, and every surface avoids ambiguous language about what each level proves.

What is on chain

Hashes only. Everything else off chain.

A buyer needs to verify a slice without trusting our records. A contributor needs to revoke without us able to deny it. That is the whole job.

One leaf per episode

A 197-byte preimage: payload hash, manifest hash, consent commitment, terms hash, capture and submission timestamps, duration, scope bits, channels, task ID, world seed (if sim), success flag, quality score. No identity, no payload, no free text. The consent commitment is salted fresh on every submission so episodes cannot be linked.

A monotonic head, not roots

The log is an RFC 6962 Merkle tree, living off chain and anchored hourly with its previous root and its size. A pile of unrelated roots proves nothing about ordering. A monotonic head with a size makes a consistency proof possible. The chain anchors the log; it is not the log.

Revocation via non-membership

Consent withdrawal needs a non-membership proof — an inclusion list cannot express absence. Revocations live in a sparse Merkle tree anchored in the same call. One extra word per anchor. The verifier reports the block at which a withdrawal became public, which is the fact a buyer's counsel needs.

Payment and terms, one transaction

The only place a chain beats alternatives. Off chain: "the buyer paid under these terms" is our word. On chain: a USDC transfer and terms hash in one call, immutable afterwards. A receipt three years later is checkable against a head that provably existed.

Chain strategy

Primary anchor on Avalanche C-Chain

The protocol is chain-agnostic; no chain ID appears in any record. Avalanche C-Chain is a good primary because USDC settlement is native and embedded. A mirror anchor on Ethereum ensures the log survives if any single chain or its RPCs disappear. Any EVM chain can carry the log; no chain is privileged in the protocol itself.

Known limitations

What the protocol does not prove

The operator can omit or refuse to anchor

Append receipts and public audit make this detectable, not impossible.

Checks are heuristics, not proof

They have recorded thresholds; they can be evaded and can err; they are evidence, not certainty.

Signatures prove authorship, not measurement

A signature proves which key signed, not what a sensor measured. captured_at, source, and embodiment are claims by the signer, not the chain.

Revocation onset is recorded; rights are not

The protocol records when consent was withdrawn. What a buyer may do after that onset is governed by the terms document, not by this protocol.

Anchors depend on chain availability

The log is verifiable only if at least one chain carrying it is reachable. The same log is anchored on more than one for redundancy.
