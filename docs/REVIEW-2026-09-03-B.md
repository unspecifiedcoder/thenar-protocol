# Strategic + architectural review B — the live product and the protocol

Second gate review, 2026-09-03, after reading https://thenar.io and every
route in its sitemap. This document answers the brief's 24 questions; the
decisions it produces are recorded as D-30…D-36 in
`docs/ARCHITECTURE-DECISIONS.md` and folded into `PLAN.md` v2.2.

---

## 1. The two systems

### A. This repository (`thenar-avax`, now v2.1 in progress)
GRASP: append-only CT log with SMT revocation; leaf codecs 0x01–0x04;
`GraspLog`/`LeafVerifier`/`LicenceRegistry`; SQLite log service; API with
uploads, ingest of LeRobot v3, org keys; checks (timing, kinematics, dedup);
badges. 22 tasks implemented, 151 Solidity tests. Never deployed to thenar.io.

### B. The live product (thenar.io — the "Axon" codebase, not in this repo)
- **What it is:** "The data foundry for physical AI." A bounty board. A
  poster escrows AVAX for a task (object, destination, instruction, room,
  difficulty, slots, reward-per-run, deadline). An operator connects a wallet
  or passkey, drives **THENAR-6** — a procedurally generated six-axis arm
  rendered in the browser with an IK solver, **no rigid-body physics** —
  and submits a run.
- **Data generated:** a trajectory (joint targets over time from the IK
  solver against a scene datum). Representation is not published beyond
  "trajectory ledger"; there is no physics, so contact never happens.
- **Scoring:** placement 55 %, smoothness 25 %, time-against-par 20 %,
  evaluated against the task datum; difficulty sets par.
- **Payment:** `AxonProtocolV2.recordRun` — "records a run and pays for it
  in one call"; reward scaled by score; unused escrow stays or locks.
- **On chain (12 contracts on Fuji):** tasks, escrow, trajectory hashes,
  policies with contributor cap tables, soulbound recorder certificates, a
  contribution counter, referrals, prize pools, a work-weighted treasury
  vote, confidential payouts, a Warp-signed "LicenceReceipt", time-boxed
  corpus access ("sells time, not rights"), a corpus manifest, a P-256
  passkey registry. Plus a transcript of a local Avalanche L1.
- **Off chain:** the trajectories themselves, the scene, the scorer, the
  site.
- **Trust assumptions:** the scorer is the operator's word; the trajectory
  hash is committed but nothing binds it to a canonical byte layout anyone
  can recompute; the "policy" that gets minted is a name, not a model.
- **Usage:** 0 tasks, 0 runs, 0 operators, 0 AVAX paid. The v1 contract
  "contains stranded funds".
- **What it is genuinely good at:** (1) it is a *complete loop* — post,
  operate, score, pay — that runs in a browser with no install; (2) the
  passkey-on-P-256 idea is the right primitive for signed capture; (3) the
  copy is unusually honest ("Nothing here is a fixture", "scores nothing and
  changes no payout").

### The comparison in one line
The live site is a **marketplace for producing simulated arm trajectories**
with settlement. The repository is **infrastructure for proving what any
dataset is**. The first has no buyer (mouse-driven, physics-free trajectories
train nothing). The second has a buyer but no supply. They are not the same
company, and only one of them survives contact with a customer.

## 2. The fundamental question — answer: **C with a controlled slice of B**

| Option | Verdict | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A. Foundry is the core company | **No** | Zero demand after a full loop was shipped; the data cannot train a policy (no physics); competitors with real hardware (XDOF, Config, Tacta) are funded 100×. |
| B. Foundry is the wedge | **No as wedge, yes as sandbox** | It cannot produce the evidence the thesis needs — a buyer paying a premium for verified *real* data. It can produce the first end-to-end L0→L1→L3 record under our control, which is worth keeping for the demo and for fixtures. |
| C. THENAR is a reference implementation of the protocol | **Yes** | The protocol serves external suppliers and buyers; THENAR runs the log, the verifier and one reference capture source (the browser arm, declared `sim`). |
| D. Two separate products | **No** | Two half-companies; the foundry has no customer to justify a P&L. |
| E. Something else | — | C, with the browser arm demoted to `/lab` as a *simulation source* and fixture generator, and the twelve Axon contracts retired to history. |

Decided on: customer demand (provenance has a legally dated buyer; the
foundry has none), capital efficiency (the arm costs nothing to keep as a
lab, a lot to make trainable), defensibility (neutrality), speed to revenue
(reports in weeks; foundry never).

## 3. Does self-generated experience create an advantage? — **Small, real, bounded**

Yes for three things, no for the big one:
- **Yes — protocol exercise.** Our own captures let us produce signed
  (L1, passkey) manifests, claims and revocations without waiting for a
  supplier. The golden demo's step 1 can run from the lab arm.
- **Yes — fixtures for the verifier.** A controlled generator makes exact
  duplicates, jittered replays, time-warps and physics-free trajectories on
  demand — the ROC fixtures T-017 synthesised by hand.
- **Yes — the first L1/passkey path.** The live site's `PasskeyRegistry`
  is exactly D-26's P-256 key; porting the idea gives the SDK a browser
  signer for free.
- **No — the flywheel.** Simulated, physics-free trajectories never enter a
  buyer's training set, so they never earn the price-premium signal, never
  populate the dedup index with anything a supplier's real episodes would
  collide with, and never make external ingestion better. The flywheel in
  THESIS §4.5 runs on *real* episodes only. Self-generated data is a
  sandbox, not a seed.

So THENAR is **both** a producer and the neutral infrastructure — but the
producer role is a lab, labelled as such, and the infrastructure role is the
company.

## 4. "Short of data, not compute" — **half true, and the wrong half**

Open supply in 2026: Open X-Embodiment >1M trajectories; LeRobot 16k+
datasets; XDOF's ABC-130K open; AgiBot World; DROID. For arms and grippers,
*quantity* is being commoditised by open releases. What remains scarce:
1. **On-target data** for a specific embodiment and task (the 5–20k
   fine-tune episodes) — a capture problem, being funded.
2. **Trustworthy, licensable data** — 10–20 % of HF robotics datasets have
   no licence; 40–160 h of counsel time per programme; EU AI Act
   enforcement since 2026-08-02; nobody can answer "what trained this".
3. **Clean data** — duplicates, replays and mislabelled sims inside paid
   deliveries.

Problem 1 is bigger and belongs to hardware companies. Problems 2 and 3 are
the ones a neutral party can own. The site's slogan sells problem 1 with a
product that solves none of the three. Retire the slogan.

## 5. The billion-dollar thesis — **Option 7, in a fixed order**

| Option | Alone | Role in the combination |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Data foundry | no buyer | lab / reference source |
| 2 Neutral provenance | necessary, not sufficient (toll booth ≈ $30–50M) | the base layer |
| 3 Trusted machine-experience infra | the umbrella name | the category |
| 4 Verification layer | the first premium; compounds with log size | the wedge's paid feature |
| 5 Rights/licensing infra | the regulatory demand driver | receipts + consent |
| 6 Exchange/settlement | small take; becomes the product only if 2–5 work | receipts on chain, revenue off chain |

**Category to own: "trusted machine experience" — provenance (2) + verification (4) + rights (5), with settlement (6) as a primitive, and the foundry (1) as a lab.** Unchanged from THESIS, sharpened: the umbrella name is "trusted machine experience", not "CT for machine experience" (which describes the mechanism, not the value).

## 7. Should the browser robot survive? — **Yes, as D + E; never as C or F**

- A (toy): unfair — it is a working capture loop.
- B (contributor acquisition): no — contributors of mouse-driven sims are not the supply the market pays for.
- **C (controlled data-generation environment): no as a product** — physics-free trajectories are not training data; making them so is the MuJoCo-WASM programme we shelved.
- **D (benchmark/fixture generator for the verifier): yes.** Deterministic, scriptable, produces every adversarial fixture class.
- **E (demonstration of provenance from capture to settlement): yes.** It is the only source THENAR controls end to end; the demo's L1 (passkey-signed capture) can be shown live.
- F (first supply-side network): no — see the counters.

Role in the architecture: `/lab/arm` is a **reference capture source of kind `sim`** that emits CaptureManifests through the same `POST /episodes` path as the SDK, signed by a P-256 passkey, badge-capped at L1 + L3, always displayed with "declared: simulation". It never pays anyone from protocol funds. Task T-038 (Phase D).

## 8–9. The claim ladder and simulation — **add a source axis; never let L-levels imply physicality**

The evidence levels answer *"how strong is the evidence that these bytes are
what the signer says?"* They never answer *"did a physical robot do this?"*
That second question is a separate axis, **source**, which is a *claim* by
the signer and is upgraded to *attested* only by specific evidence:

| `source` (declared) | Meaning | What upgrades it from "declared" to "attested" |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `sim` | rendered/simulated; no physical robot | nothing needed; it is the weakest claim and is never contested |
| `teleop_sim` | a human drove a simulated robot | same as `sim` |
| `teleop_real` | a human drove a physical robot | L2 attestation of the robot controller **and** `sim_signature.v1 = pass` **and** (later) `sensor_consistency.v1 = pass` |
| `autonomous_real` | a policy drove a physical robot | same as `teleop_real` |
| `mixed` | dataset mixes sources | never attested as a whole |

What each stage of the browser-arm path actually evidences:
- Browser trajectory → **canonical capture**: bytes + manifest; `source: "sim"`.
- → **L0**: existed by block *b*, unchanged, consent state.
- → **L1**: the passkey holder signed it before logging.
- → **L2**: *not reachable* for the browser arm (no hardware controller). A phone passkey in a secure element would attest the *signer's device*, not the robot — display must say so.
- → **L3**: timing/kinematics/dedup can run; `sim_signature.v1` trivially passes with "declared sim".
- → **L4**: the operator's history — of simulated runs.

Rules now in PLAN (D-30): every surface shows `source` with the word
**"declared"** unless the attested condition holds; `sim`/`teleop_sim` can
never display as physical; the CorpusManifest carries `sources[]` and a
corpus mixing declared-real and sim episodes is labelled `mixed`.

## 10. The architecture diagram — **correct with two amendments**

The brief's diagram is right in shape. Amend: (1) the three inputs are not
peers — they are *declared sources* that share one canonical capture and one
evidence ladder; (2) L2 sits beside L1 (attestation strengthens signing), not
after it, and only physical sources can reach "attested physical". Rendered
in PLAN §3. Rights/settlement branch is correct; the "neutral experience log"
box is the CT log + SMT already built.

## 11. L3 — validated as the first premium, not yet as a moat

Evidence-backed ranking of what buyers pay for (from supplier QA practice,
public dataset licence reviews, and how labs filter OXE-style corpora):
1. **Near-duplicate / replay detection** — directly reduces what they pay per episode; the only check that compounds with log size. Premium-capable.
2. **Task compliance / success labelling** — the live site's scorer is a primitive version; labs re-label anyway; moderate premium.
3. **Quality scoring (smoothness, time)** — used for filtering; low premium alone.
4. **Sim-vs-real** — insurance against fraud; premium only once a fraud case is public.
5. **Timing/kinematics sanity** — hygiene; expected, not paid for.
6. **Sensor consistency** — niche until video-conditioned policies dominate.

Experiments (write into the 90-day plan):
- **E1 (dedup value):** run `dedup.v1` over five public HF datasets; publish the duplicate rate per dataset. If any exceeds 2 %, that number is the sales deck.
- **E2 (willingness to pay):** offer three labs a report on a corpus they are evaluating; price the report at $5k; count conversions.
- **E3 (supplier pull):** offer two suppliers a "pre-checked" badge on their listings; measure whether buyers ask for it unprompted within 60 days.
- **E4 (premium):** in E2 deals, ask for two prices — with and without L3 — and record the delta. This is THESIS §4.5's flywheel variable.

## 12. First customer — a named role, not a category

**The ML lead who owns fine-tuning data budget at a Series A–C robotics
company that buys third-party episodes**, plus their outside counsel. They
feel the pain (duplicates and unlicensed data waste a $200k–$2M line), they
control the budget, they experience today's failure (a spreadsheet and a
vendor's word), and they can adopt in a week (send us the dataset). Suppliers
are the second customer, adopting to close those buyers.

## 13. The wedge — **existing datasets first; THENAR-generated data is the rehearsal**

The "THENAR-generated first" path cannot produce E2/E4 evidence (nobody buys
sim trajectories), so it cannot tell us whether the company exists. The
"existing datasets first" path produces buyer evidence in weeks and fills the
log with real episodes. Sequence: lab arm → rehearse the loop internally (no
customer) → public datasets → reports → suppliers → SDK → network.

## 14. The existing payment system — **a useful primitive, not the product**

`recordRun` binding (hash, task, wallet, score, payment) is the same instinct
as our `license()` receipt. Keep the primitive (`LicenceRegistry`): terms +
content commitment + payment in one call. Drop everything that turns it into
a marketplace (escrow bounties, prize pools, referrals, treasury votes). Yes,
one neutral system can hold rights + provenance + verification + settlement —
the log already holds the first three as leaves and the registry holds the
fourth — but revenue in year one is invoiced reports, not on-chain take.

## 15. Blockchain — unchanged, with the live site's excess named

On chain: anchors (canonical head + timestamps), terms hashes, sealed corpora,
receipts. Off chain: everything else. Avalanche C-Chain primary (USDC,
finality, payments narrative), Ethereum mirror, chain-agnostic leaves. The
live site's Warp-signed receipts, local L1, confidential payouts and
soulbound tokens are removed: none serves a relying party.

## 23. Final company test

- **What is THENAR today?** Two unconnected artefacts: a live bounty board that pays for physics-free arm trajectories (zero runs) and a repository with a correct provenance log, contracts and checks (no deployment).
- **What should it become?** The trusted-machine-experience layer: neutral provenance + verification + rights for physical-AI data, with settlement as a primitive.
- **Is the browser robot important?** As a lab and demo source, yes; as a business, no.
- **Is the data foundry important?** No. Retire the slogan and the marketplace mechanics.
- **Is the provenance protocol important?** It is the company.
- **Wedge:** Provenance Reports on real datasets changing hands.
- **Platform:** the log + verifier + registry that suppliers write to and buyers' counsel read.
- **Moat:** neutrality → relying-party habit → dedup index that only the neutral log can build → lineage switching costs.
- **Who pays / why:** ML leads and counsel at data-buying robotics companies, to make a $200k–$2M purchase defensible and to stop paying for duplicates; suppliers, to close those deals.
- **Why not Hugging Face / Encord / an internal DB / SaaS?** HF hosts, it does not attest or check; Encord and suppliers are parties to the sale; an internal DB is the buyer's own word; a normal SaaS can do everything except be *un-forkable and neutral* — the one property a relying party needs and the only thing the chain provides.
- **Why blockchain?** One canonical head no party can fork; a timestamp nobody administers; a receipt neither side can revise. Nothing more.
- **Why Avalanche?** USDC-native settlement, cheap finality, a payments story counsel recognise; the protocol mirrors elsewhere and depends on none of it.
- **Network effect:** every logged real episode makes every future duplicate check stronger, and only the neutral log sees everyone's episodes.
- **Strongest path to $1B:** provenance toll → verification premium → lineage receipts for models → pooled fleet data under rights, with THENAR as the rights engine.
- **What makes it a $20M SaaS:** buyers accept L0 reports without paying for L3; suppliers self-attest and counsel accepts it; no premium ever appears.
- **The deciding experiment:** E4 — the price delta a buyer pays for an L3-checked corpus versus the same corpus at L0, measured in three real deals within 12 months.

## 24. Verdict

**GREEN — build now:** T-012, T-020, T-021 (running), T-025, T-027, T-030, T-033, T-040, T-016/T-036/T-019/T-026 (running).
**YELLOW — after frontier review:** enabling `fail` on dedup/consistency/sim (FD-1/FD-2); T-039 scorer thresholds (FD-5); terms text (FD-4); any §9–§12 change.
**RED — not until customer evidence:** T-022 SDK, T-023 attestation, T-037 devices, T-038 lab arm recorder (all Phase D — after E2 yields ≥3 paid reports), any marketplace mechanic, any token, any L1, MuJoCo physics for the arm.

**Will GREEN converge without drift?** Yes — with v2.2: the source axis is now in the schema and wording (D-30), the browser arm has a defined, capped role (D-31), the live site's mechanics are explicitly retired (D-33), Phase D is fenced behind evidence (D-34), and the STOP conditions now name source/attestation semantics. Nothing a Sonnet agent can build from a GREEN task can make a simulated trajectory look physical, pay anyone from protocol funds, or reintroduce a marketplace.
